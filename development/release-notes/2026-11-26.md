## Grammars whose words are foams

A record of events in time makes a typed 2-complex, generated one event at a time, and syntaxis can now learn its grammar. The new module `foams` reads each event as a production whose word is its star: the participants it consumes, those it leaves or begins, and the interfaces it opens and closes. It keys each star four ways up to isomorphism (the whole star, the action, what it consumes, and that in its surroundings), and its curvature sums exactly to the complex's Euler characteristic, so one can say which kinds of event generate the topology. Independent events commute, so a record's order is a trace whose Foata steps it gives.

The grammar is learned at three levels by a hierarchical Dirichlet backoff, with its escape in closed form and its concentrations by leave-one-out. It is judged on events it has not yet seen: held out after a date, or prequentially, each event priced by the grammar of every event before it. The second keeps a source that begins partway through a record from passing for a failure of the grammar. Grammars of one record are compared as a partial order, with no single score. The participants' lives, read as words, are compressed by Re-Pair, so a run of events that several participants share appears as a rule. 25 checks, among them a foam worked by hand, χ = Σκ on 200 random records, and a context planted in events that only the context-sensitive grammar finds.
