# Release Notes: 8 November 2026

## 1. syntaxis Founded: Grammars Whose Words Are Geometric

syntaxis is a new foundation for grammars whose words are geometric: graphs in the plane and complexes of triangles ([decision 0002](../decisions/0002-scope-boundaries-and-dependencies.md)).

- **Learn a grammar from one structure.** What repeats, up to rotation, scale and position, becomes a rule, chosen by how much of the description it saves. The grammar found replays its input exactly. A self-similar structure of 243 nodes reduces to an axiom of three, one rule for each depth.
- **Parse by it.** A structure is analysed into a forest that says which rule derives which part, how much is covered and whether it is derived. A copy turned, scaled and moved parses as the original.
- **Make it probabilistic and context-sensitive.** Rules of one shape merge into categories, recursive on self-similar input, and are weighted by their use. Conditioned on their parent's category, and refusing to cross what is drawn, they sample new structures of many sizes.
- **Read triangles as letters.** Polygons and the faces of plane graphs become complexes of triangles, each a letter by its angles. The grammar of their gluing gives the letters' entropy and what a neighbour tells about a letter, and grows new patches, conditioned on their size since its growth is subcritical. A triangle rewritten by Pachner's, the midpoint's and Sierpinski's moves has its derivation recovered from the final triangles alone.
- **A theory note** states each construction with its hypotheses and proofs, and what was found while checking it: node replacement cannot share the corners of Sierpinski's gasket, keys rounded at a tolerance are sensitive to it, and growth from counts is subcritical. 70 checks.
